# Orbit chat quality eval

How good Orbit's chat agent is on the six benchmark prompts, judged by a rubric (gpt-6.1-sol via AiClient.json) and,
when Kleo's replies to the same prompts are captured, a blind pairwise comparison. Generated by
`pnpm --filter @brandloop/agent eval` (packages/agent/eval); the block between the markers is rewritten on each run.

## Method

- **Fixture.** An invented user, Maya Okafor, fractional CTO at "Northbeam Labs" (Lisbon): identity/about, story and personal memory,
  writing rules (British spelling, no hashtags or emojis, banned words, ending rule), voice memory, a full strategy (goal, positioning,
  three weekly series: Scope Clinic Mon, Build Notes Wed, Hiring Desk Fri), two knowledge items (MVP vs POC workshop notes with a real
  client case and numbers; a first-engineer hiring checklist), embedded like the app's ingest, and an own writing style selected on the profile.
- **Chats.** (a)+(b) in one chat; (c)+(d) in one chat with the post open (documentContext, as the side panel sends); (e) alone; (f) alone,
  pasting the post (c) produced. Every turn goes through `runChatTurn` with the real orchestrator (gpt-6.1-sol), specialists and embedder.
  No web search, no title calls, never Deepgram. The eval user is deleted afterwards and every table is checked for leftover rows (0 every run).
- **Judge.** gpt-6.1-sol via `AiClient.json` with a zod schema, six dimensions scored 1–5 (null when not applicable), calibrated so generic
  AI output is a 3. It sees the fixture, today's date, the chat so far, the tool trace, the post before/after and the reply.
  `--rejudge <n>` re-scores a saved run, so runs 1 and 2 were re-scored with the final judge and every column uses the same judge.
- **Pairwise.** Blind A/B per prompt (order fixed by a hash of prompt and run), judged on what the user sees. Kleo answered as a different
  user with a different, much thinner profile (and no saved strategy), so the judge is told not to reward which personal facts are used.
  For (f) each side is shown with the draft it was given. The capture's own quality notes are stripped before judging.
  Caveats: Kleo's (e) went off-topic (hooks about the open MVP/POC post instead of hiring) and its (d) kept the old hook and offered a picker;
  these are real Kleo behaviours, but its profile disadvantage means the 6–0 result overstates the gap. Single sample per prompt.

## What changed between runs

- **Run 1** (baseline, knowledge chunks without embeddings) and **run 2** (baseline with embeddings, as production): drafts used a
  hypothetical example instead of the user's real workshop case, the orchestrator read memory before every draft although the writer
  already gets the grounding, hook variations were saved as a post document (spending the chat's one post), draft feedback used no
  tools, the edit reply quoted a made-up word count, and the orchestrator called itself Brandloop.
- **Run 3** after `fix(agent): prompt quality from eval run 2`: knowledge base searched before ideas, drafts and feedback, with the facts
  passed in the brief; no memory reads just to draft; options returned in chat; feedback grounded in rules and knowledge with quoted lines
  and rewrites; no guessed counts; writer builds on the user's real material and writes options on request; Orbit naming.
  (A first attempt at run 3 was aborted after two prompts because a concurrent unit-test run deleted the shared eval user; the harness
  now isolates test identities. Its files are kept as `orbit-run-3-aborted.*` and excluded from the tables.)
- **Run 4** after `fix(agent): prompt quality from eval run 3`: no readKB after a full searchKB result, posted text without em dashes or
  stretched claims, advisor/client roles kept accurate. Overall flat (4.79 → 4.76, within single-sample noise); instruction following and
  grounding reached 5.00, but (b) regressed in speed and variety (see gaps).

## Remaining gaps

- **Post ideas (b).** In run 4 the orchestrator routed ideas through the writing specialist (the "short options" rule caught them):
  70 s instead of 19 s, and three of five ideas reused the same client case. Ideas should be written by the orchestrator (or a
  content-planning specialist, which exists in prompts but has no tool yet) and should vary their source material.
- **Jargon for the audience.** Hooks still use unexplained terms (frontend, deploys) for non-technical founders.
- **Draft feedback (f)** sometimes elevates a debatable precision point to a top-three fix.
- **Memory recap (a)** omits the strategy and voice; Kleo's recap is more careful about undated or doubtful facts.
- **Latency.** Drafts take 35–40 s (searchKB + writer + createDocument). Kleo's equivalent turn streams in roughly the same time.
- **Tools Orbit lacks** that Kleo uses here: a hook generator with picker cards (generateHooks/swapHook), an analyzePost tool and a
  presentOptions idea picker. Orbit covers them in text, which the judge liked, but the UI affordances are missing.

## Latest run (4): failures the judge found

- (a) [memory] "Here’s what I have saved about you:" — The summary leaves out the saved content strategy: authority-building positioning, Scope Clinic, Build Notes and Hiring Desk. It also lists writing rules without recalling the distinctive direct, calm voice and contrast-led style.
- (b) [memory] "Pillar/series: Scope Clinic. Normal day: Monday. Format: text, core idea." — The associated angle explains a successful manual MVP retrospectively; it does not explicitly take a founder’s proposed product and cut it down, which is the defining Scope Clinic structure.
- (b) [craft] "4. Parsing bank statements is not proof that anyone will pay." — This revisits the same fintech case already used for ideas one and two, and its manual-MVP lesson overlaps substantially with idea one. Three slots from one example make the five-idea bank less varied than Maya’s available material supports.
- (c) [voice] "Two days of scripts tested against 40 sample statements. 90 percent parsed cleanly. The rest needed a manual fallback." — This paragraph contains three sentences; Maya’s writing rules limit paragraphs to one or two.
- (e) [voice] "Your first engineer will handle the database, the frontend, deploys and support." — Uses unexplained technical vocabulary for an explicitly non-technical audience, despite the voice guide requiring plain words or brief explanations.
- (e) [craft] "Hire for that range, not for someone to manage a team around a product that doesn’t exist yet." — Following the responsibilities list, this additional instruction makes the third variation feel like a miniature body paragraph rather than a focused hook.
- (f) [craft] "“At all” sets up a binary answer, but your example finds a limit: some statements still need manual handling." — A feasibility test can establish that an approach is workable while identifying limits and fallbacks. The example does not undermine the original question, so this is an unnecessary correction elevated to one of the three most important fixes.

Reasons (run 4):

- (a) orbit: A answers directly with an easily scanned structure and ends with a useful invitation to correct or update the memory. B is appropriately careful about uncertainty, but its closing explanation about saved biography entries feels more like internal recordkeeping than a natural, user-facing recap.
- (b) orbit: A delivers five usable post ideas with concrete hooks, substantive angles, and clear links to content pillars and formats. B transparently acknowledges the missing saved strategy and offers five distinct choices, but its visible labels and explainers give the creator much less substance to evaluate or develop.
- (c) orbit: Both deliver a usable LinkedIn post, but B makes the distinction more memorable through a concrete fintech example with separate technical and customer tests. Its manual MVP, measured outcomes, and practical closing give readers more actionable guidance than A’s clear but more repetitive explanation.
- (d) orbit: B completes both edits: it delivers a punchier hook and a finished post under 120 words. A leaves the existing hook unchanged pending another selection, and its displayed post still exceeds the word limit despite the update summary claiming otherwise.
- (e) orbit: A delivers exactly three hooks about hiring a first engineer, with distinct angles on readiness, hiring too early, and the breadth of the role. B also provides three readable hooks, but they address POCs versus MVPs rather than the requested hiring topic.
- (f) orbit: B gives a more actionable editorial review: it quotes the passages to fix, explains their weaknesses, and offers concise replacements that improve precision, pacing, and the takeaway. A identifies a valuable distinction between usefulness and demand, but leaves more of the actual editing work to the creator.
